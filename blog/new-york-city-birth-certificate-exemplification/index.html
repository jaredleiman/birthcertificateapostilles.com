New York City Birth Certificates: The Exemplification Step Explained

New York City birth certificates travel a three-link chain no other state uses: city exemplification, county clerk certification, then the state apostille. What each link is and why skipping one ends in rejection.

Request a Quote

A birth certificate from New York City is not apostilled the way almost any other American birth certificate is. Between the certificate and the apostille sit two extra links that exist nowhere else in quite this form: a Letter of Exemplification from the city, and a certification by the New York County Clerk. Applicants who do not know about the exemplification step usually find out about it from a rejection.

New York City is its own vital-records world

Births in the five boroughs are recorded by the New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, not by New York State. The apostille itself is issued by the New York Department of State, and the Department of State authenticates signatures it can verify. The signature on a city-issued certificate is not the end of that chain — which is what the next two links are for.

What a Letter of Exemplification is

When you order a long-form New York City birth certificate for international use, the Health Department can issue it with a Letter of Exemplification: an accompanying page in which the city attests that the certificate is a true copy of the record on file. The letter is part of the certificate order — it is requested at the time the certificate is ordered, and it is the piece the rest of the chain is built on. Ordering the certificate without it produces a document that cannot complete the apostille chain.

The county clerk link

With the exemplified certificate in hand, the New York County Clerk certifies the exemplification. Only then does the document go to the New York Department of State, which issues the apostille on the strength of the clerk's certification. Three links — city, county, state — where most states have one. The same structure applies to New York City death certificates.

Where applicants go wrong

The recurring errors are all versions of skipping a link. Ordering the short-form certificate instead of the long form. Ordering the long form without the exemplification letter, and assuming the letter can be added later. Sending a city certificate straight to the Department of State. Or assuming a certificate issued by New York State's Department of Health follows the same path — it does not: state-issued records signed by the Director of Vital Records go directly to the Department of State without the county clerk step, which is why "New York" instructions that do not distinguish city from state cause so much confusion.
