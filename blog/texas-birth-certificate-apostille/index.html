Texas is the second-largest state in the country by population, and a Texas birth certificate apostille is among the most frequently requested vital record authentications in the U.S. Whether your certified copy was issued by the Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS Vital Statistics) or a local registrar in Harris County, Dallas County, Bexar County, Tarrant County, Travis County, or El Paso County, an apostille is what makes that Texas vital record legally valid in foreign countries.

What an Apostille Does for Your Texas Birth Certificate

An apostille is an international certification under the Hague Apostille Convention, recognized in over 120 countries, that confirms your birth certificate is an authentic Texas state record. With an apostille attached, foreign governments, civil registries, courts, and universities accept your Texas birth certificate as official proof of birth, identity, and U.S. citizenship, with no need for embassy or consular legalization.

The Most Common Reasons People Apostille a Texas Birth Certificate

Dual citizenship and citizenship by descent

Texans with Mexican, German, Czech, Polish, Italian, or Irish ancestry regularly pursue second citizenship through their family line, and the apostilled birth certificate is the foundational identity document in every one of those applications. Texas's deep multigenerational ties to Mexico make dual nationality registration one of the single biggest drivers of birth certificate apostille requests in the state.

Marriage abroad

Getting married in Mexico is practically a Texas tradition, and destination weddings there, along with marriages in Italy, France, Colombia, the Philippines, and elsewhere, typically require an apostilled birth certificate for the foreign marriage application.

Foreign residency and long-stay visas

Retirement abroad, family reunification with a foreign spouse, and long-term residence permits commonly call for an apostilled birth certificate as proof of identity and family relationships.

Registering with a foreign civil registry

Children born in Texas to immigrant parents often need an apostilled birth certificate to be recorded in the parent's home country, a prerequisite for that country's passport, inheritance rights, or benefits.

Inheritance, property, and legal matters overseas

Estate proceedings, land ownership claims, and family court matters in foreign jurisdictions require authenticated proof of identity and lineage, and the apostilled birth certificate is usually the first document requested.

International adoption, study, and work

Adoption dossiers, foreign university enrollment, professional licensing, and certain overseas employment applications also frequently include an apostilled birth certificate.

Why Texas Demand Keeps Growing

Between the largest U.S.–Mexico border of any state, major international hubs in Houston and Dallas–Fort Worth, a huge energy-sector workforce with overseas ties, and fast-growing immigrant communities, Texans are constantly moving across borders, for family, work, marriage, and second citizenship. Every one of those journeys tends to start with the same document: a Texas birth certificate that's been apostilled for international use.
